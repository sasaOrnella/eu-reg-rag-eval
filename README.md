# EU Regulation RAG Evaluation

Evaluating how retrieval method (BM25, dense, hybrid) and chunk size affect evidence recall over the English GDPR and EU AI Act texts, under a fixed context-token budget. Results are compared across question types; answer faithfulness is a secondary outcome evaluated at the generation stage.

> This system is a research/engineering exercise. It does not provide legal advice.

## Research question
On the English texts of the GDPR and the EU AI Act, how do retrieval method (BM25, dense, hybrid) and chunk size affect evidence recall under a fixed context-token budget, and does this effect differ by question type (single-hop, multi-hop, conditional, numeric)? Unanswerable questions have no gold evidence, so they are analysed only at the generation stage (does the system correctly abstain?). Answer faithfulness is a secondary outcome with a fixed generation model.

## Hypotheses (write BEFORE running experiments)
- H1: Holding the total retrieved context fixed (about 2,000 tokens, so k = budget / chunk size), evidence recall is an inverted U in chunk size: chunks of roughly 800–1,200 characters outperform both small (≤ 400 characters) and large chunks (≥ 2,000 characters). Sizes stay within the embedding model's input limit, except a whole-article baseline evaluated with BM25 only. A monotonic trend falsifies H1.
- H2: (a) On questions with high lexical overlap with their evidence, BM25's recall is at most 0.05 below dense retrieval's (margin fixed in advance). (b) On low-overlap questions and multi-hop questions, dense or hybrid retrieval has higher recall than BM25. (c) Hybrid is not significantly worse than the better single method on any question type (95% bootstrap CI).
- H3 (recall vs. answer quality): On multi-hop questions, gains in recall translate into smaller gains in answer correctness than on single-hop questions (“retrieved does not equal answered correctly”). This hypothesis can be tested only after the generation stage and is stated in advance.

## Deviations / Pilot Analysis Decisions

1. For H2(a), Dev Pilot grouped lexical overlap scores below 0.30 as Low and scores of 0.30 or higher as High. The cutoff comes from the [question-writing rules (R-Q3)](eval/question_rules.md) and was adopted for this analysis without an explicit preregistered H2 threshold.
2. H2(a) retains its original 0.05 recall difference margin. The Dev Pilot has yet to establish the predicted margin result.
3. H2(c) had no prespecified non-inferiority margin. A nonsignificant difference cannot establish non-inferiority.
4. All H1 and H2 analyses to date are exploratory Dev Pilot analyses. A confirmatory evaluation remains pending.
5. Of the 33 answerable Dev questions, 25 were AI-drafted. Human review is pending, with no complete verification record for those 25 questions.

## Limitations
The initial 20-question evaluation set is intended to test the pipeline, not to support robust performance conclusions. A larger set (target: at least 150 questions) is needed for comparative claims. Other limitations include a single regulatory domain, annotation ambiguity, and potential judge bias. Some questions may have multiple valid evidence passages, while the current annotation format records only one gold evidence reference; these alternatives should be documented during error analysis. Unanswerable questions are assessed separately at the generation/abstention stage.
